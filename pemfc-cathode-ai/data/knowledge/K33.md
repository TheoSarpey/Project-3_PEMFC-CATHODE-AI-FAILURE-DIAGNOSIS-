title: Adsorption-energy references and free-energy corrections

Adsorption energies of OH, O and OOH are usually referenced to gas-phase water and hydrogen, which avoids the poorly described O2 molecule in common functionals. Converting an adsorption energy to a free energy adds zero-point energy and entropy terms; commonly used totals are about 0.35 eV for OH, 0.05 eV for O and 0.40 eV for OOH, but the numbers depend on the functional, on solvation and on coverage, so check them against your own setup. Mixing energies computed with different references, functionals or codes in one table is a frequent source of false trends.
