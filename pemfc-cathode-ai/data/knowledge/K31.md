title: Computational hydrogen electrode (CHE) method
topic: dft-method

The computational hydrogen electrode method avoids calculating a solvated proton and an electron. At 0 V against the standard hydrogen electrode, a proton-electron pair is in equilibrium with half an H2 molecule, so its free energy is taken from H2. An electrode potential U then shifts the free energy of each reduction step by eU. Reaction free energies come from DFT total energies plus zero-point and entropy corrections. The method gives thermodynamic trends, not rates: it ignores activation barriers, explicit solvent structure and the interfacial electric field unless they are added on purpose.
